# The Tongue is a hand-rolled damped spring, not a rope constraint

The Tongue applies a one-way damped spring force along the vector between the hero and the Anchor, written by hand against `glam`, with no physics crate in the Core Sim. It is not a distance constraint and there is no maximum length. Force magnitude grows with stretch, so a long Tongue pulls harder than a short one, and a hero who moves away from an Anchor after attaching loads the spring and is then slung back toward it. The reference feel is Spider-Man's webs.

This replaces an earlier version of this ADR that specified a position-based distance constraint at a bounded radius. That framing was wrong: a rope forbids a radius but has no direction or strength to scale, so it cannot express a pull that varies with length, and it cannot express two Tongues whose pulls add and cancel as vectors.

## Why no physics crate

A reader will expect Rapier2d and may try to add it, so the reasoning is recorded. Rapier is not `glam`-only, so it breaks the engine-free boundary in ADR-0001. Its cross-platform determinism is not guaranteed, which undercuts the deterministic fixed step in ADR-0002. Its spring and rope joints are also the wrong primitives: we need a one-way spring with no push phase and a force law we tune directly. The simulation is small, being one hero body, up to two springs, low gravity, static terrain, and some Enemies.

## Consequences

Swing is **emergent**, not modelled. The spring pulls inward while tangential momentum is conserved, and under the deliberately moon-scale gravity the result is spiralling orbital arcs that read as swinging. Release exits on the tangent. Nothing in the simulation represents a pendulum.

Two Tongues are no longer a constraint problem. Forces sum as vectors, so there is no infeasible configuration, no solver iteration count, and no jitter: pulls in the same direction double, opposed pulls cancel. This supersedes the over-constraint consequence recorded in ADR-0006.

A Tongue on a Movable Anchor pulls both bodies by their mass ratio, so a hero tongued to an Enemy and to terrain at once is a genuine three-body system that must be solved as one, not as two independent pairs. A Tongue attaching to a Movable Anchor always attaches to its exact center of mass.

The rest boundary is geometry-bound rather than freely tuned: `REST_LENGTH = 2.0 * PLAYER_RADIUS`. Below it the Tongue is slack and applies nothing, and past it Hooke's law acts on the Stretch. Equilibrium hang length is therefore `REST_LENGTH + m*g/k`, so a stronger pull hangs closer to the boundary and `REST_LENGTH` is a hard floor no hero gets inside. No separate short-range rule is needed, because Hooke's law already tapers force continuously to zero at the boundary.

**A Swing does not decay, and that follows from the force law rather than from a tuning choice.** Damping opposes radial velocity, meaning motion along the Tongue, while a Swing is almost entirely across it. The radial mode is therefore damped and settles to the hang length when a hero drops straight down a Tongue, but the angular mode is barely damped at all and a hero arriving sideways keeps orbiting more or less indefinitely. This is physically right for a spring that only resists stretching, and it is desirable: momentum is the resource the game is built on. It will nonetheless surprise anyone who raises `DEFAULT_DAMPING` expecting a Swing to die out, because that knob cannot reach the orbit. Anything that should bleed angular momentum needs its own mechanism and a decision behind it. Discovered while implementing ticket #12.

**The #8 prototype's loaded-Stretch return speeds are not reproducible at `DEFAULT_DAMPING`, though its ordering and its gain structure are.** That table records 18.81 units per second returned from a 3 unit Stretch and 88.48 from 15, which are 99 and 93 percent of the undamped ceiling `stretch * sqrt(k/m)`. Those two fractions disagree with each other, and neither is consistent with `zeta` 0.237: a quarter cycle of a damped oscillator keeps `exp(-zeta*(pi/2)/sqrt(1 - zeta^2))`, about 68 percent. Measured through the `World`, the same two Stretches return 13.85 and 69.19, which are both 1.07x that damped prediction and so agree with each other and with the physics. The prototype's absolute numbers therefore look like a harness that damped less than the shipped constants do, and its 1 unit row is an artifact outright: that row has zero Stretch, so its 6.96 is entirely free fall its harness allowed before measuring.

What survives, and is what the table was cited for, is structural: return speed is proportional to Stretch, so five times the Stretch returns 5.00x the speed here against the prototype's 4.70x, and a deliberately loaded Stretch is worth an order of magnitude over a short Attach. Anyone re-tuning `DEFAULT_DAMPING` against the prototype's absolute figures will chase a number the constants cannot produce. Discovered while implementing ticket #13.

**The same prototype's two-Tongue force table does reproduce exactly, and why it does belongs next to why its speed table does not.** Every row of it was measured at zero velocity, where the damping term `damping * radial_vel` is identically zero, so the figures are Hooke's law and vector addition and nothing else. At `k` 40 with the Anchor 10 units out the Stretch is 9, so one Tongue pulls 360.000, two on one Anchor 720.000, two exactly opposed 0.000, and two perpendicular 509.117. Measured through the `World` by inverting the integrator's position step, all four land within 1e-3 of those figures, and the aligned pair is the only one with any residual at all. The rule that falls out: a figure that prototype measured at rest is safe to assert against, and one it measured in motion is not. Discovered while implementing ticket #14.

**The force ceiling is per Tongue rather than per hero, so two Tongues can pull at twice it.** `DEFAULT_FORCE_CEILING` clamps each pull as it is evaluated, and the clamped pulls are then summed, so a hero braced between two Anchors 2000 units apart feels two 1200 unit pulls and not one. That is a consequence of forces summing rather than a separate choice: a cap on the total would mean a Tongue that weakened because another one existed, and aligned pulls would stop doubling, which is the property this ADR exists to provide. The bound on the hero's acceleration from Tongues alone is therefore `MAX_TONGUES * DEFAULT_FORCE_CEILING / mass`, which is the number to size a speed cap or a Kill Bound against. Discovered while implementing ticket #14.

The numerics were measured in the Tongue prototype for ticket #8 rather than guessed. The integrator question escalated into its own decision, ADR-0007, because explicit Euler turned out to be unusable rather than merely imprecise. A force ceiling is required, but for gameplay rather than stability: force grows linearly with Stretch and there is no maximum length, so an uncapped attach at 1000 units produces a peak speed above 5000 units per second. Damping must stay underdamped, roughly `zeta` between 0.1 and 0.25, since an undamped spring never settles and a critically damped one destroys the snap the mechanic exists for.
